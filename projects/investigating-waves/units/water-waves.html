Unit 01 · Splash, ripple, surf

Water Waves

Drop a pebble. Watch the ripples race outward. Now here's the wild part: the water barely went anywhere — but the wave traveled clear across the pond. Let's figure out how that works. 🌊

Concept 01 · What is a wave?

A wave carries energy,
not water.

If you drop a rock in a pond, ripples spread out in a perfect circle. But a rubber duck floating nearby? It only bobs up and down. It doesn't ride away with the wave. That's because the wave is moving energy through the water, not the water itself. Try it 👇

Concept 02 · Anatomy of a wave

Every wave has the same parts.

The top of a wave is the crest. The bottom is the trough. The height from middle-to-crest is the amplitude (how tall!). The distance from crest to crest is the wavelength (how wide). How many waves pass by each second is the frequency (how fast). Play with the sliders and watch.

A tiny surfer 🏄 rides the wave. Watch how the amplitude makes his ride taller, the wavelength stretches the waves apart, and the frequency controls how fast they zoom by.
Lab 01 · Ripple tank

When two waves meet…
something wild happens.

Scientists use ripple tanks — shallow tanks of water — to study waves. When two wave sources wiggle at the same time, their ripples overlap. Sometimes they team up and make giant waves (bright). Sometimes they cancel out and make glassy calm (dark). That's called interference. Drag the two droppers around and see for yourself. 💧💧

Lab · Two-Source Ripple Tankdrag the dots · scroll to adjust wavelength
Try this: drag the two droppers close together, then far apart. With one source, you get nice concentric circles. With two, you see bright rays of big waves and dark rays of calm — like a stripey starfish. That's how light and sound interfere, too!
Lab 02 · Surf's up

Why do waves break at the beach?

Out in deep water, waves are gentle rolling hills. But when a wave reaches shallow water, the bottom drags on the ocean floor. The top of the wave keeps going fast — until it tips over and crashes. 🌊💥 Control the wave size and the sea floor, then make 'em break.

Try this: make the slope steeper. See how the waves pile up higher and break harder? A tsunami is what happens when a whole lot of energy reaches shallow water at once.
Did you know?

Six wild wave facts.

🚀

Tsunamis are faster than a jet.

In the deep ocean, a tsunami can travel 500 mph — as fast as a 747. They only get huge once they reach shallow water.

🗻

The tallest wave EVER.

In 1958, a wave in Lituya Bay, Alaska reached 1,720 feet — taller than the Empire State Building!

🦆

Rubber ducks are scientists.

In 1992, a shipping container of 28,000 rubber ducks spilled into the ocean. Scientists tracked where they washed up to map ocean currents!

🌙

Tides are also waves.

The moon's gravity pulls the ocean into a giant wave that sweeps around Earth twice a day. You call it "the tide."

🎵

Water waves + sound waves = same idea.

Sound and light also travel in waves — just through the air and empty space instead of water. The math works the same way.

🏟️

"The Wave" at a stadium is a REAL wave.

People stand up and sit down in a pattern, and the motion travels around the stadium — even though nobody moves seats. That's exactly how water waves work!
