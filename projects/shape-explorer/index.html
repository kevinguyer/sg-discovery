What Is a Polygon?

A polygon is a closed, flat (2D) shape made of straight line segments. The word comes from Greek: poly (many) + gon (angles). Below — try the Polygon Forge and build any polygon from 3 to 24 sides.

The 3 Rules: To be a polygon, a shape must be ① closed (no gaps), ② made of straight lines only (no curves), and ③ flat (2-dimensional).

✔ These ARE Polygons

  • ✔ Triangle (3 sides)
  • ✔ Quadrilateral (4 sides)
  • ✔ Pentagon (5 sides)
  • ✔ Hexagon (6 sides)
  • ✔ Heptagon (7 sides)
  • ✔ Octagon (8 sides)
  • ✔ Nonagon (9 sides)
  • ✔ Decagon (10 sides)

✘ These are NOT Polygons

  • ✘ Circle — curved, no straight sides
  • ✘ Oval — curved, no straight sides
  • ✘ Open shape — not closed
  • ✘ Shape with a curved side
  • ✘ 3D shapes (cubes, spheres, etc.)

Regular vs. Irregular Polygons

In geometry, "regular" has a very specific meaning — it does NOT mean "normal" or "ordinary." A regular polygon has all sides equal AND all angles equal. Try the lab!

⚖️ The Regularity Meter
Drag any corner. Watch the meter — 100% means perfectly regular. The closer to regular, the brighter the glow!

Regular polygon = ALL sides are the same length AND ALL angles are the same size. If either one is different, the polygon is irregular.

Key Idea: A shape can be irregular and still be a polygon. "Irregular" just means the sides or angles aren't all the same — the shape isn't broken or wrong!

Combo Labels! Each triangle gets one name from each group. Example: a right scalene triangle has a 90° angle and no equal sides. An acute isosceles has all angles < 90° and two equal sides.

📐 Note about trapezoids: We use the inclusive definition — a trapezoid has at least one pair of parallel sides. That means parallelograms, rectangles, rhombuses, and squares are also trapezoids! (Some textbooks use an "exclusive" definition that says exactly one pair — we don't.)
