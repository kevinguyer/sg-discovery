Every tricky shape is a simple shape in disguise. Slice a parallelogram and it becomes a rectangle. Stack two triangles and they become a parallelogram. Grab the scissors — the area never changes, only the shape does. ✂️

The one rule of Shape Slicer

When you cut a shape into pieces and move the pieces around, the amount of space inside stays exactly the same. Mathematicians call that amount the area. That means you can turn a shape you don't know how to measure into shapes you do — rectangles and triangles — and the answer will still be right.

For teachers

Shape Slicer builds the idea behind 6.G.A.1 (find areas by composing into rectangles or decomposing into triangles and other shapes) and reinforces 5.G.B.3–4 (properties of two-dimensional figures). Nothing is graded or saved — every page is a fresh sandbox.

  • Units 01–03 each prove one area formula by cutting and rearranging, before the formula is ever written down.
  • Unit 04 is open-ended: good for pairs, and for asking "is there a way with fewer cuts?"
  • Unit 05 is the application: composite figures, add-and-subtract strategies, and a short exam with typed answers.
  • Works offline from a file — open index.html from a USB drive or the class share.
