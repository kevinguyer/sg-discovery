Concept 01 · Conservation

Cut it a hundred times. The area never changes.

Here's the idea that makes all of Shape Slicer work: a straight cut divides a shape into pieces whose areas add up to the original. Move them, spin them, flip them — the total stays. Mathematicians call this the conservation of area. Watch the "Total area" number below. Try to change it. You can't.

Bonus

Six wild dissection facts.

🧩

They're called dissections

Puzzles where you cut one shape and rebuild another are called dissection puzzles. People have been inventing them for over 1,000 years.

🔺

Triangle → square in 4 pieces

In 1902, Henry Dudeney showed that an equilateral triangle can be cut into just 4 pieces that hinge together into a perfect square. It's still famous.

✅

Any polygon → any other

The Wallace–Bolyai–Gerwien theorem says any two straight-sided shapes with the same area can be cut into pieces that rebuild each other. Always!

🐍

Tangrams are dissections

The Chinese tangram puzzle is a square cut into 7 pieces. Those 7 pieces can rebuild thousands of shapes — animals, letters, people — all with the same area.

🧊

It breaks in 3D

Cubes and other 3D solids don't always work this way. Max Dehn proved in 1900 that you can't cut a cube into pieces that rebuild a regular tetrahedron of the same volume.

🥇

Fewest cuts is a real sport

Puzzle fans compete to find the minimum number of pieces for each dissection. Some records have stood for a century — and some were beaten last year.
