Unit 02 · The hall of mirrors

Triangle Twins

Every triangle has a secret twin. Spin the twin around, snap them together, and you get a parallelogram — which means a triangle is always exactly half.

▼ SCROLL TO MEET THE TWINS
Concept 01 · The twin trick

Every triangle is half of a parallelogram.

Make an exact copy of any triangle. Spin the copy 180° (a half-turn, upside down) and slide it against the original along one side. The two fit perfectly and make a parallelogram. You learned last unit that a parallelogram's area is base × height. The triangle is one of two identical halves, so its area is half of base × height.

Half of what you already know

Parallelogram = base × height. The triangle is half of that parallelogram — same base, same height.

Area of a triangle = base × height ÷ 2

Some people write it as ½ × base × height. Same thing. Build it yourself below.

Lab 02 · Another way

The Half-Height Fold

Here's a second proof, with scissors this time. Cut the triangle along the dotted line at half its height. Then cut the little top triangle straight down through its tip. Spin the two tiny pieces into the corners and you get a rectangle that's base × half the height. Same answer, new route.

Lab 03 · The big secret

Which side is the base?

Trick question: any side can be the base. But then the height has to be measured from the opposite corner, straight down to that base (or to the line it sits on). Drag the corners around and click a side to make it the base. The area comes out the same every time.

Bonus

Six wild triangle facts.

🧱

The strongest shape

Push on a square and it leans into a parallelogram. Push on a triangle and it refuses to budge. That's why bridges and roofs are full of triangles.

🔺

Three twins, not one

A triangle has three sides, so it has three different parallelogram twins — one for each side you attach the copy to. All three have the same area.

🇪🇬

Ancient rope-stretchers

Egyptian surveyors used knotted ropes to make right triangles and measure fields after the Nile flooded. Triangle area = your share of farmland.

🧩

Every polygon is triangles

Any shape with straight sides can be chopped into triangles. Computer games draw everything — dragons, mountains, faces — out of millions of tiny triangles.

🎪

Half-height trick, tent-style

Cut at half height and fold the top down. Tent makers and paper-airplane folders use this exact move to turn a triangle into a rectangle of cloth.

🤯

Same base, same height, same area

Slide the top corner of a triangle sideways — as far as you like. As long as it stays the same height, the area never changes. Try it in Lab 03!
