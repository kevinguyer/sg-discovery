Chapter 3 · 1607 through the American Revolution · VS.3

Jamestown, 1607

Three small ships. One hundred and four men and boys. A swampy island on a river they named for their king. The first English colony in North America that lasted almost didn't. This is the story of why they came, why they chose that spot, how they nearly died, and who kept them alive.

Section 1 · Reasons for colonization · VS.3a, VS.3c

Why would anyone leave England for this?

In the early 1600s England was crowded, running short of timber and other natural resources, and jealous of Spain, whose colonies were sending home shiploads of silver. A group of wealthy men formed a business, the Virginia Company of London, and asked the king for permission to plant a colony. They hoped it would make them rich. It nearly ruined them instead.

Section 2 · Choosing the site · VS.3b

Why here?

The Company's sealed instructions said: go up a river, far enough inland that Spanish ships can't surprise you; pick a spot you can defend; and don't take land the Indigenous people are already using. On April 26, 1607, the ships reached Cape Henry. For two and a half weeks they explored the river, weighing their choices.

Section 3 · Hardships and changes · VS.3d, VS.3e

How Jamestown almost died, and didn't.

Most of the first colonists were gentlemen who had never farmed, and workers who came for gold, not corn. They drank bad river water, got sick in the summer heat, and fought each other. Within nine months two out of three were dead. Each disaster forced a change, and each change is a reason the colony survived.

Section 4 · The Powhatan and the English · VS.3d

Neighbors, traders, rivals.

The colonists landed in the middle of Chief Powhatan's chiefdom, about 15,000 people in thirty tribes. At first the Powhatan traded corn for copper, beads, and tools, and taught the English what grew here. Pocahontas carried food and messages. But the English kept taking land, and the friendship wore thin. We know this story mostly from the English side; the Powhatan never wrote theirs down.

Section 5 · Three arrivals · VS.3f, VS.3g

1619: the year that changed everything.

Twelve years after the landing, three things happened in about twelve months that shaped Virginia and America for centuries: the colonists met to make their own laws; the first Africans were brought here against their will; and shiploads of English women arrived to make the colony a home.
