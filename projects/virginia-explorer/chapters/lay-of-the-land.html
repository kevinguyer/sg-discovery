Chapter 1 · Physical geography · VS.1

The Lay of the Land

Before the people, before the history, there was the land: five regions from the ocean to the mountains, four great rivers, a bay, a swamp, and six neighbors. Everything that happened in Virginia happened somewhere. Let's learn where.

Section 1 · Relative location · VS.1a

Where in the world is Virginia?

Virginia sits on the east coast of the United States, about halfway between Maine and Florida. It is bordered by five states, the nation's capital, the Chesapeake Bay, and the Atlantic Ocean. Mapmakers describe where a place is by what is around it. That's called relative location.

    Section 2 · The five regions · VS.1b

    Five regions, from the sea to the mountains.

    Geographers divide Virginia into five geographic regions, running in bands from east to west. Each has its own topography (the shape of the land), its own rivers, and its own jobs. Regions are lines people drew, not walls, but the land really does change as you travel west.

    Section 3 · Bodies of water · VS.1c

    Four rivers, a bay, a lake, and a swamp.

    Water decided where Virginians lived. The Chesapeake Bay was a safe harbor and a pantry. Four big rivers, the James, York, Rappahannock, and Potomac, were highways into the land, and every one of them ends in the Bay. Where the rivers tumble over the Fall Line, boats had to stop, so cities grew.
